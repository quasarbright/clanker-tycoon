# The game's humor

Mike, 2026-11-18, on Pam's business-name and game-over mocks: "this is so fucking funny, this
exactly the type of humor I want ... this is the gold standard."

## The rule

**The punchline is one of the game's own mechanics** (shipping, bins, instructions, the lender,
the robots taking you literally), said in a dry, official voice. Never a generic business or
office joke that could be in any game.

## Gold standard

Headlines Mike loved (the front page when a business goes under, `src/headlines.js`):

- "Clank & Sons Ships Its Last Box (It Was Empty)"
- "Clank & Sons Reputation Found at Bottom of Bin 2"
- "Robots of Clank & Sons Finally Given Clear Instructions: 'Leave'"
- "Clank & Sons Paint Machine Outlasts Clank & Sons" (round 2, Mike: "ok")
- "Lender Collects His 10% of Clank & Sons: $0" (round 2; Mike liked "Lender takes 10% of
  nothing" and asked for it in real headline form)

Each one is about something you actually did on the floor: boxes that ship, the bins you store
things in, and the instructions you write.

The business license (the screen where you name your business, `index.html` `#licenseScreen`).
Mike: "perfect amount of on-theme and cute, love how it's official-sounding but ridiculous."

> CERTIFICATE OF BUSINESS. Widget District · Form 7-B · Quarter 1.
> This certifies that the business known as ___ may make, box and ship widgets, and is
> responsible for every robot it puts on the floor.
> Stamp: APPROVED, capital: $100. The lender: "Lovely name. I'll remember it."

## What didn't land

From the same batch, Mike passed on the tabloid front page and seven other headlines: a quoted
angry client, the lender's snacks, "says no one", missing widgets, "every deadline", a forklift,
and a sad drawing of a box. They're jokes about business in general, not about this game.

Round 2 (2026-11-18), twelve more: Mike kept two (above) and dropped ten: "most of those aren't
even headlines wtf ... the rest are not headlines, or just not funny or clever." Dropped: still
waiting for red; red boxes to a blue order, calls it "close"; owner yells, "No Change to
Instructions"; runs out of a supply that never runs out; pauses shift, never unpauses; final box
had two widgets; trains robot to be Fast, robot leaves first; plan passed only the dry run; three
robots, one thought between them. Near miss: "Robot Comes to Desk With a Question; Desk Gone".
Naming a mechanic isn't a joke by itself; those read as a game log, not a newspaper.

## How to use it

- **A headline must read like a real newspaper headline:** a subject, an active verb, newspaper
  phrasing (present tense, dropped articles, a quote or a colon reveal). Read it aloud as if it
  were on a newsstand; if it sounds like a status message or a patch note, it's not one.
- **And it must be clever,** a twist or reveal, not a description of a mechanic. The mechanic is
  the setup; the punchline is a turn on it (the box was empty, the clear instructions are "leave",
  the machine outlives the company).

- Write new jokes from a mechanic the player has just seen work (or fail).
- Keep the voice official and deadpan. Let the absurd part be the facts.
- Fewer jokes, better ones. When unsure, leave it out: the floor and the robots' own voices carry
  most of the comedy (see CLAUDE.md, "Workers").
